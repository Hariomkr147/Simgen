What to observe

  • Every carbon always shows exactly 4 bonds and every hydrogen exactly 1 — count the dot pairs in electron-dot mode.
  • Moving n up by 1 adds exactly one CH2 group: the formula grows by CH2 and the molar mass by 14 u every time.
  • Boiling point rises gradually and smoothly with n — a gradation in physical properties along the series.
  • For n = 4 and n = 5 the isomer slider changes the shape (branching) but not the formula C4H10 / C5H12.
  • Switching to alkene or alkyne removes 2 H each time, because a double or triple bond uses more of carbon's 4 valences.

Common misconceptions

  • “Carbon forms ions (C4+ or C4−).” It does not; it shares electrons, because gaining or losing 4 electrons is energetically unfavourable.
  • “Isomers are the same compound drawn differently.” Propane's chain drawn bent is still propane; true isomers (n ≥ 4) have different connectivity and different properties.
  • “Homologues differ by one atom.” They differ by a whole CH2 unit, 14 u.
  • “A double bond is stronger, so it has more hydrogens.” A double bond uses two valences of each carbon, so alkenes have fewer H than alkanes.
