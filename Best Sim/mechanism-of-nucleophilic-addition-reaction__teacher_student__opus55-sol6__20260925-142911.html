What to observe

  • CN⁻ attacks the electrophilic carbon, not oxygen. The C=O π-bond electrons move to O.
  • The planar sp² carbonyl centre becomes a tetrahedral sp³ alkoxide intermediate. Fast protonation gives the cyanohydrin.
  • Above about pH 9, [CN⁻] rises sharply, so the rate rises.
  • At the same conditions, the illustrative rate order is HCHO > CH₃CHO > CH₃COCH₃: alkyl groups reduce the carbon’s δ⁺ character and hinder attack.
  • Raising temperature increases k exponentially.

Activation energies are illustrative, not exact experimental data. The model holds the HCN reservoir at 1.0 M and treats protonation as instant.

Conceptual simulation only: HCN is highly toxic. Do not attempt this reaction.

Check your understanding

1. Which step is rate-determining in HCN addition?
  1. Protonation of alkoxide
  2. Attack of CN⁻ on carbonyl carbon
  3. Loss of water
  4. Protonation of carbonyl O

Answer: B. The slow step is formation of the tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate; protonation is fast.

2. Why does a trace of base speed up the reaction?
  1. It protonates C=O
  2. It raises [CN⁻] from weakly ionised HCN
  3. It lowers T
  4. It changes the product

Answer: B. HCN is a weak acid; base shifts ionisation so that more CN⁻ nucleophile is present.

3. Why is propanone less reactive than ethanal?
  1. Two alkyl groups add steric hindrance and reduce the δ⁺ on C
  2. Propanone has no C=O
  3. Ethanal is a stronger acid
  4. Propanone is sp³

Answer: A. The +I effect of two alkyls lowers electrophilicity, and their bulk hinders the approaching nucleophile.
