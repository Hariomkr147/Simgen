Governing equations

x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ)
v(t) = −Aω sin(ωt + φ)
a(t) = −ω²x(t)   ·   F = −kx
ω = √(k/m)   ·   T = 2π√(m/k)
ν = 1/T
K = ½mv²   ·   U = ½kx²
E = K + U = ½kA²

Velocity leads displacement by π/2 in phase; acceleration is opposite to displacement. ω is in rad/s, while ν is in Hz.

What to observe

  • Increasing A does not change T.
  • Quadrupling m doubles T; quadrupling k halves T.
  • Acceleration always points toward the mean position and is opposite to x.
  • Speed is greatest at x = 0; acceleration magnitude is greatest at x = ±A.
  • K and U exchange energy while E stays constant.

Check your understanding

1. If the mass is made 4 times larger, what happens to the period T?

Halves · Doubles · Quadruples · Unchanged

Reveal answer

Doubles. T = 2π√(m/k), so T ∝ √m and √4 = 2.

2. At x = +A, what is the acceleration?

0 · +ω²A · −ω²A · −Aω

Reveal answer

−ω²A. Since a = −ω²x, acceleration has its greatest magnitude here and points toward the mean position.

3. At which position is kinetic energy maximum?

x = +A · x = 0 · x = A/2 · x = −A

Reveal answer

x = 0. U = ½kx² is zero there, so all of E = ½kA² is kinetic energy.
