Governing equations
H = 2n + 2 − 2k  ·  M = 12n + H u
For alkanes: bonds = 3n + 1
Successive homologues: ΔM = 14 u (one CH₂ unit).

A double bond contains two shared pairs; a triple bond contains three. Every C has valency 4 and every H has valency 1.

What to observe

  • Every carbon has exactly four bonds and every hydrogen has one. Switch to electron-dot mode to count shared pairs.
  • Increase n by one: the formula gains CH₂ and molar mass rises by 14 u.
  • Straight-chain alkane boiling points show a gradual increase along the series.
  • At n = 4 or 5, change the isomer: the branching changes, but C₄H₁₀ or C₅H₁₂ does not.
  • Changing alkane → alkene → alkyne removes two H atoms at each step: multiple C–C bonds use more of carbon’s four valences.

Check your understanding

1. What is the molecular formula of the alkane with 7 carbon atoms?

C7H14 · C7H16 · C7H12 · C7H18
C7H16. Alkanes follow CnH2n+2; for n=7, H = 2×7+2 = 16.

2. How many covalent bonds (shared pairs) are there in one molecule of ethane, C2H6?

6 · 7 · 8 · 14
7. One C–C bond plus six C–H bonds = 3n+1 = 7 shared pairs, i.e. 14 shared electrons.

3. Butane and 2-methylpropane (isobutane) both have formula C4H10. They are called:

Homologues · Allotropes · Structural isomers · Ions
Structural isomers. They have the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms: straight and branched chains.

Useful distinctions

Covalent bond: a shared pair of electrons, not a carbon ion.

Homologues: successive members differ by CH₂ (14 u), not by one atom.

Structural isomers: the same formula with different connections between atoms—not merely the same chain drawn bent.
