Governing equations

CO = HR × SV / 1000 (L/min)

T = 60 / HR (s)

Atrial systole: 0 to 0.1875T
Ventricular systole: 0.1875T to 0.5T
Diastole: 0.5T to T

Blood pumped per ventricle in t s = HR × SV × t / 60 (mL)

What to observe

  • Atria contract before ventricles (watch the phase bar).
  • The right side carries only deoxygenated blood (blue); the left side only oxygenated blood (red).
  • Valves close to stop backflow when ventricles contract: AV valves shut, semilunar valves open.
  • Follow one dot: blood passes through the heart twice in one full circuit (body → heart → lungs → heart → body).
  • Raising HR or SV raises cardiac output — the dots in the vessels move faster.

Common misconceptions

  • The pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood, not deoxygenated.
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart; they do not always carry oxygenated blood (pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood).
  • Ventricle walls are thicker than atria because ventricles pump blood to distant organs or the lungs.
  • Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix in the human heart — the septum keeps them apart.
