What to observe

  • Atria contract before the ventricles in every beat.
  • Tricuspid and bicuspid valves close when the ventricles contract.
  • Blood turns red in the lungs and blue in the body tissues.
  • Blood passes through the heart twice per full circuit.
  • Raising the heart rate shortens T and raises cardiac output.

The model's equations

Beat period: T = 60 ÷ HR seconds
Cardiac output: CO = HR × SV mL/min
Place within a beat: φ = (t mod T) ÷ T
Phases: atrial contraction 0 ≤ φ < 0.125; ventricular contraction 0.125 ≤ φ < 0.5; relaxation 0.5 ≤ φ < 1.
Aortic pressure: during ventricular contraction, P = 80 + 40 × sin[π(φ − 0.125) ÷ 0.375] mm Hg; otherwise, P = 80 mm Hg.

Contraction is also called systole; relaxation is called diastole. A sphygmomanometer measures blood pressure, often written 120/80 mm Hg (systolic/diastolic).

Check your understanding

1. Which chamber pumps oxygenated blood to the body?

Right atrium · Right ventricle · Left atrium · Left ventricle

Left ventricle. The thick-walled left ventricle pumps blood into the aorta.

2. HR = 75 beats/min and SV = 70 mL. What is the cardiac output?

5.25 L/min · 1.07 L/min · 145 mL/min · 52.5 L/min

5.25 L/min. 75 × 70 = 5250 mL/min, which is 5.25 L/min.

3. What prevents backflow from the ventricles into the atria?

Arteries · Valves · Capillaries · Lungs

Valves. Valves close when the ventricles contract, keeping flow one-way.
