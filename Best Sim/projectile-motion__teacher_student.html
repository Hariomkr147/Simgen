What to Observe

  • Constant Horizontal Velocity: The horizontal vector arrow (light blue) never changes length or direction because horizontal acceleration is zero.
  • Highest Point Velocity: At the peak of trajectory, v_y becomes zero, but v_x is unchanged (≠ 0), so the projectile remains in motion horizontally.
  • Optimal Range: Range peaks at 45°. Angles symmetrical about 45° (such as 30° and 60°, or θ and 90°−θ) produce the exact same horizontal range R.
  • Speed Dependency: Doubling initial speed u quadruples both maximum height H and range R (proportional to u²), while time of flight T doubles.
  • Gravity Effect: Reducing g (e.g., Moon) stretches the parabola dramatically in both height and range.

Check Your Understanding

1. A ball is launched at 20 m/s at 30° on Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²). What is its time of flight?
Correct Answer: 2.04 s
Explanation: T = 2u sin(θ)/g = 2 × 20 × sin(30°) / 9.8 = 20 / 9.8 = 2.04 s.
2. At the highest point of its path, the projectile's velocity is:
Correct Answer: purely horizontal, equal to u cos(θ)
Explanation: v_y = 0 at the top, but v_x = u cos(θ) is unchanged because there is no horizontal acceleration.
3. Two projectiles are fired with the same speed at 35° and 55°. Which statement is true?
Correct Answer: Both have the same range but 55° goes higher
Explanation: sin(2 × 35°) = sin(70°) = sin(110°) = sin(2 × 55°), so ranges are equal. However, maximum height H depends on sin²(θ), which is larger for 55°.
