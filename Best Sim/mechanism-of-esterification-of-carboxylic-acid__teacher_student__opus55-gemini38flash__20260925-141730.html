What to Observe

  • Equilibrium Limitation: With 1:1 equimolar reactants (n = 1) and no water removal (f = 0), yield levels off near 66.7% where Q approaches Kc = 4.
  • Role of Acid Catalyst: Raising [H⁺] drastically speeds up the arrival at equilibrium, but does not alter the final equilibrium concentrations or yield. With [H⁺] = 0, the uncatalysed rate (k₀ = 0.001) makes the reaction virtually inert.
  • Le Chatelier's Principle: Shifting the position of equilibrium to push yield past 67% can be achieved either by taking alcohol in large excess (raising n) or by continuous removal of water (raising f via a Dean-Stark trap or conc. H₂SO₄ drying agent).
  • Acyl–Oxygen Cleavage: Notice the red oxygen atom (¹⁸O isotope tracer). It stays anchored to the ethyl group and ends up in the ester molecule, demonstrating that water forms from the carboxylic —OH and acidic proton, not from the alcohol's —OH.

Check Your Understanding (NCERT Class 12)

1. With a 1:1 mole ratio of ethanoic acid and ethanol with Kc = 4, what is the maximum theoretical ester yield?
Correct: 66.7%. Solving Kc = x² / (1 − x)² = 4 gives x / (1 − x) = 2, so x = 2/3 ≈ 66.7% yield at equilibrium.
2. In an isotopic tracer study using ¹⁸O-labelled ethanol (C₂H₅¹⁸OH), where does the ¹⁸O appear in the products?
Correct: Ester. Nucleophilic attack by C₂H₅¹⁸OH on the protonated carbonyl carbon leads to acyl–oxygen cleavage: the acid loses its —OH group as water, keeping the red ¹⁸O atom inside the ester.
3. What dual role does concentrated H₂SO₄ play in Fischer esterification?
Correct: Protonates the C=O and removes water. Protonation renders the carbonyl carbon strongly electrophilic, while its dehydrating action absorbs water, shifting the equilibrium forward according to Le Chatelier's principle.
